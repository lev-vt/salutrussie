AN IMPORTANT FEATURE OF RUSSIAN PROPERTY TRANSACTIONS

Estate agents, notaries and lawyers have different roles

Property buyers in Russia do not always understand the role of each professional. This is particularly important for foreign buyers accustomed to a different conveyancing system in their own country.

The involvement of a notary or estate agent does not replace comprehensive, independent legal due diligence carried out in the buyer's interests.

Estate agent

An estate agent's main role is to find properties, arrange viewings and help the parties agree purchase terms. Legal services may be offered separately, but finding a property does not itself constitute a full legal review.

Notary

A notary authenticates the transaction and checks its legality, the parties' intentions and authority, the seller's ownership and statutory restrictions. A notary acts impartially, not as the buyer's own lawyer. Notarial authentication does not guarantee that the transaction will never be challenged.

Buyer's lawyer

Your lawyer conducts comprehensive, independent due diligence specifically in your interests, examines ownership history, third-party rights, litigation and insolvency risks, recommends safeguards and assesses whether you should proceed with the purchase.

On a notary's powers: Fundamentals of Russian Legislation on Notaries, Articles 54–55.

MORE THAN 20 YEARS OF LEGAL PRACTICE

Why choose a property lawyer with litigation experience?

Registering ownership is only the beginning. What matters is ensuring that the property you buy remains yours.

Even after ownership has been registered, a transaction can be challenged. Buyers may face claims from heirs, former spouses, creditors or previous owners. In some cases, they risk losing the property and struggling to recover the money they paid.

A lawyer with hands-on litigation experience understands what can give rise to a claim, what evidence may be needed in court and how to protect buyers before they commit to a transaction.

If we identify significant risks, we explain them and recommend safeguards, including advising against the purchase where appropriate.

What our legal due diligence covers

Ownership and property history

We examine records from the Unified State Register of Real Estate (EGRN), title documents, the basis on which the property was acquired and the available history of ownership transfers.

Seller and third-party rights

We review authority to act, family and inheritance circumstances, and the rights of minors and other persons relevant to the transaction.

Litigation and insolvency risks

We examine available information about court cases, enforcement proceedings, insolvency and circumstances that could lead to a transaction being challenged.

Encumbrances and restrictions

We check registered restrictions, mortgages and pledges, attachment orders, prohibitions and other matters affecting the acquisition and use of the property.

Land and restrictions for foreign buyers

We check the land category, permitted use, recorded plot boundaries in the EGRN, applicable territorial restrictions and special requirements for foreign buyers. Since 1 March 2025, missing boundary information in the EGRN may be grounds for suspending registration of rights to a plot or a property situated on it (Article 26(1), points 21.1 and 21.2, of Federal Law No. 218-FZ).

Contracts and payments

We analyse the contract, advance payment or earnest money arrangements, payment procedures and ownership registration. We recommend terms that reduce the buyer's risks.

The scope of due diligence depends on the type and history of the property, the documents available and the circumstances of the transaction.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a lawyer if a notary authenticates the transaction?

Yes, if you want an independent assessment of the risks specifically for you as the buyer. Notarial authentication and comprehensive legal due diligence serve different purposes. A notary checks legality within the scope of their powers, but this does not prevent subsequent challenges.

Is an extract from the property register enough?

No. An extract contains important information about registered rights and restrictions but does not reveal every circumstance that could give rise to litigation.

Can a buyer lose property even after ownership has been registered?

Yes, in cases provided for by law. For example, disputes may concern the invalidity of the transaction or recovery of property. The outcome depends on the circumstances, legal grounds and protections available to a good-faith purchaser.

When should I start legal due diligence?

Before making an advance payment or paying a deposit, and before signing documents that create obligations. This lets you negotiate while you still have the option not to proceed.

Can I request due diligence only if I already have an estate agent?

Yes. We can independently review the property, documents and transaction terms without becoming involved in the property search.

Can you guarantee that a transaction will never be challenged?

No. Even a notarised and registered transaction can become the subject of a dispute. Our role is to identify risks that can reasonably be checked, recommend ways to reduce them and help you make an informed decision.
