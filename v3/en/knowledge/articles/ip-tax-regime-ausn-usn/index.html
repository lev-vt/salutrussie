USN or AUSN for foreign sole traders: taxes, contributions and comparison for 2026–2027

A comparison of the simplified and automated simplified tax systems for foreign entrepreneurs: rates of 6%, 15%, 8% and 20%, insurance contributions, thresholds, VAT and practical calculations.

Lev VtiurinLawyer and founder of SalutRussie · In legal practice since 2005SalutRussie editorial teamLast reviewed: 24 November 2026

In brief. Under the simplified tax system (USN), the standard rates are 6% of revenue or 15% of revenue less expenses. Under the automated simplified tax system (AUSN), the rates are 8% or 20%. But the choice depends on more than the tax rate: insurance contributions, VAT, employees and eligibility restrictions can change the calculation entirely.

USN and AUSN: the main differences

Both regimes simplify taxation for small businesses. Under USN, entrepreneurs maintain their own tax records and file an annual return. Under AUSN, the Federal Tax Service (FNS) calculates tax using information from banks, cash registers and the taxpayer's online account.

Tax thresholds that are indexed annually must be checked for the relevant year. AUSN is available in regions participating in the pilot scheme, which under current legislation runs until 31 December 2027.

How insurance contributions affect your total tax burden

Under USN, individual entrepreneurs generally pay fixed mandatory pension and health insurance contributions for themselves: RUB 57,390 for the full 2026 year and RUB 61,154 for the full 2027 year. Where qualifying income exceeds RUB 300,000, an additional contribution of 1% of the excess is payable, subject to the statutory cap. How income is determined depends on the tax base chosen.

Sole traders using revenue-based USN without employees may offset their eligible own contributions against tax without the 50% cap, reducing tax to zero if applicable. For sole traders with employees, the reduction is capped at 50% of the tax. Under the revenue-less-expenses option, contributions are deducted as expenses under the applicable rules.

Under AUSN, sole traders' own contributions and ordinary employee insurance contributions are not payable. Employers remain liable for a fixed occupational accident insurance contribution (RUB 2,959 for the full 2026 year) and employee personal income tax obligations. The higher AUSN tax rate may therefore be offset by lower mandatory costs.

Practical example: a sole trader without employees

Assume an entrepreneur operates throughout 2026, uses the standard USN rate of 6% or AUSN rate of 8%, has no employees, pays no VAT on ordinary transactions and meets all conditions of both regimes. The additional contribution is offset against USN for the same year; it cannot be deducted again the following year.

If your business has substantial expenses

For retail, manufacturing and other businesses with significant costs, consider the revenue-less-expenses option. For example, with steady monthly revenue of RUB 250,000 and allowable expenses of RUB 175,000, AUSN tax amounts to RUB 15,000 per month (RUB 180,000 over 12 months). Under the revenue-based option, it amounts to RUB 20,000 per month.

If allowable expenses rise to RUB 240,000 per month with the same revenue, the ordinary calculation produces RUB 2,000 of tax, but the minimum tax is RUB 7,500 (3% of monthly revenue). AUSN minimum tax is calculated monthly, whereas USN minimum tax is calculated annually. Do not substitute a simple annual formula for an actual AUSN calculation: seasonality and loss carry-forward may affect the result.

Expenses must comply with the rules of the chosen regime and be supported by documents. Not every bank account debit qualifies as a deductible expense.

VAT under USN in 2026–2029

Federal Law No. 228-FZ of 4 July 2026 retained the USN revenue threshold for VAT exemption at RUB 20 million in 2026–2029. For 2027, the exemption generally applies if revenue in 2026 did not exceed RUB 20 million. If the threshold is exceeded during the year, the obligation to calculate VAT arises on the first day of the following month.

When VAT becomes payable, taxpayers may, subject to eligibility, choose the general rates (the standard rate is 22%, with applicable input tax deductions) or special rates of 5% and 7%, under which input VAT generally cannot be deducted. In 2026, the threshold between the special rates is RUB 272.5 million, and the maximum USN revenue limit is RUB 490.5 million. Exceeding the exemption threshold does not itself result in loss of USN eligibility.

AUSN generally provides VAT exemption for ordinary transactions, subject to exceptions such as imports and tax agent obligations. If your turnover is approaching RUB 20 million, compare the regimes taking into account VAT and the terms of your customer contracts.

Regional USN rates and relocation

Regions may reduce standard USN rates where the statutory conditions are met. From 2026, you must also consider the list of activities and criteria established by the Russian Government, as well as regional legislation.

Moving to a region with a lower tax rate does not immediately entitle you to that rate. Since 2025, a rule has applied for three consecutive tax periods, including the year of relocation, under which the corresponding rate of the former region applies if the new rate is lower. Holders of a temporary residence permit (RVP) must also consider immigration restrictions. Fictitious relocation creates tax and immigration risks.

Is AUSN suitable for a foreign entrepreneur?

Foreign nationality alone does not prevent a registered Russian sole trader from using AUSN. However, the regime has strict eligibility criteria: participation of the region in the pilot scheme, revenue, employee numbers, accounts with authorised banks and the absence of prohibited activities.

In particular, you cannot employ individuals who are not Russian tax residents, pay income to individuals in cash or act as an agent, commission agent or attorney under an agency arrangement. Selling your own goods through an online marketplace does not in itself mean you are acting as an agent. If you have foreign accounts or make cross-border payments, check eligibility in advance.

AUSN does not require an ordinary annual return, but entrepreneurs must monitor the classification of bank transactions and the accuracy of tax data. For immigration procedures from 2027, establish separately which documents can be used to prove income.

How to switch to USN or AUSN

A newly registered sole trader may notify the FNS of their choice of USN or AUSN within 30 calendar days of tax registration, provided the conditions of the regime are met. An existing sole trader generally switches to USN at the beginning of the following year.

Since 2025, taxpayers using USN or the professional income tax regime (NPD) may switch to AUSN from the first day of a month by submitting notification through their online account or an authorised bank by the last day of the preceding month. A switch during the year restricts voluntary changes of tax regime for 12 calendar months. Separate rules apply in other situations.

Before switching, check all restrictions and calculate the expected burden using your projected revenue and expenses. Official FNS service for AUSN →

Key takeaway

USN is often suitable for entrepreneurs with steady income, while AUSN may be convenient for smaller businesses seeking to reduce reporting obligations. But no single regime is best for everyone. Consider contributions, expenses, employees, VAT and restrictions on business activities.
