Starting a business in Russia: should a foreign entrepreneur choose an LLC or sole trader status?

How to start a business in Russia as a foreign national: comparing limited liability companies and sole traders, director costs, insurance contributions, taxes and registration requirements.

Lev VtiurinLawyer and founder of SalutRussie · In legal practice since 2005SalutRussie editorial teamLast reviewed: 24 November 2026

Planning to start a business in Russia? Begin by choosing between a limited liability company (LLC, Russian OOO) and registration as an individual entrepreneur, or sole trader (IP). This decision affects your business structure, liability, taxes and monthly costs. Foreign entrepreneurs must also take their immigration status into account.

Business opportunities in Russia

Russia is a large and diverse market. Opportunities exist in retail and trade, services, information technology, manufacturing, agriculture and other sectors. Foreign entrepreneurs can draw on their professional experience, knowledge of overseas markets and business connections.

There are opportunities for small family businesses as well as larger projects. Choosing the right legal structure and estimating its ongoing costs in advance are essential.

LLC or sole trader: the main differences

Limited liability company (LLC) — the most common form of commercial company in Russia. It is suitable for businesses with partners, attracting investors, building a team and scaling operations. An LLC can also be established by a single founder.

Individual entrepreneur (IP), or sole trader — an individual registered to carry on business. This is a simpler structure for independent work, a small shop or providing services. However, it does not suit every project: sole traders cannot have co-founders or issue ownership interests, and some activities require a different legal structure.

How much does it cost to run a business?

Before registering, compare both taxes and recurring mandatory costs. Some obligations continue even if the business has not yet generated revenue.

Sole trader costs

Under most tax regimes, sole traders pay annual mandatory pension and health insurance contributions for themselves. In 2026, the fixed contribution for a full year is RUB 57,390, rising to RUB 61,154 in 2027. Where annual income exceeds RUB 300,000, an additional contribution of 1% of the excess is payable under the applicable rules, subject to the statutory cap.

Fixed contributions are generally payable even if there is no income. Exceptions apply, including for sole traders using the automated simplified tax system (AUSN). Employing staff creates additional payroll and insurance contribution costs.

LLC costs

A company generally has higher administrative costs, including accounting, reporting, director-related expenses and banking services. Where payments are made to directors and employees, take salaries, personal income tax and insurance contributions into account.

From 2026, commercial companies are subject to a minimum insurance contribution assessment base for their director, equal to at least the federal statutory minimum wage for a full month, even if no payments were made. At the standard rate of 30% in 2026, this amounts to RUB 8,127.90 per month (RUB 97,534.80 for a full year). These are insurance contributions alone, excluding the director's salary and other expenses. Special tax regimes and reduced contribution rates may change the calculation.

Important: an LLC does not necessarily pay more tax than a sole trader. Compare the overall burden, including taxes, contributions, running costs and how the owner receives money from the business.

How to choose a tax regime

Both LLCs and sole traders can use the simplified tax system (USN) or the automated simplified tax system (AUSN) if they meet the eligibility criteria. Sole traders may also use the patent tax system for qualifying activities.

Some regimes allow insurance contributions to reduce tax payable or be deducted as expenses. AUSN has special rules for insurance contributions and reporting. Where foreign ownership, employees, growing revenue or VAT are involved, check the availability and conditions of each regime.

It is better to compare several options using your projected revenue and expenses before registering. Learn more: USN and AUSN for entrepreneurs →

What foreign entrepreneurs need to consider

To register as a sole trader, a foreign national generally needs the right to reside temporarily or permanently in Russia: a temporary residence permit (RVP), a temporary residence permit for educational purposes (RVPO) or a permanent residence permit (VNZh).

In most cases, an LLC can be established without an RVP or VNZh. A Russian co-founder is generally not required. However, special restrictions apply to foreign ownership and certain business activities. If you plan to manage the company personally or work in it in Russia, check the immigration requirements separately.

Where to start

Decide whether you will work independently or with partners, whether you need a team and what ongoing costs you are prepared to bear. Then choose your business structure, tax regime and registration procedure.
