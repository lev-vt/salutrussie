Foreign income and overseas bank accounts in Russia: taxes and reporting

How foreign nationals living in Russia can determine their tax and currency residence, declare overseas income, notify the tax authority of foreign accounts and avoid double taxation.

Lev VtiurinLawyer and founder of SalutRussie · In legal practice since 2005SalutRussie editorial teamLast reviewed: 24 November 2026

In brief. If you live in Russia but receive salary, payment for services or other income from abroad, Russian tax obligations may arise regardless of the country in which your bank account is held. Tax residence and residence for currency control purposes must be assessed separately.

Tax residence and currency residence are different concepts

As a general rule, Russian tax residence is determined by actual presence in Russia for at least 183 calendar days during 12 consecutive months. For the final calculation of personal income tax for the year, status is assessed at the end of the tax period, taking statutory exceptions into account.

Residents for Russian currency control purposes include Russian citizens and foreign nationals permanently residing in Russia under a permanent residence permit (VNZh). A temporary residence permit (RVP) or a visa does not in itself make a foreign national a currency resident.

Which income is taxable in Russia?

As a general rule, Russian tax residents pay personal income tax (NDFL) on income from both Russian and foreign sources. Non-residents pay tax on income from Russian sources, subject to special rules.

The country in which a bank account is held does not determine the source of income. For example, remuneration for work performed in Russia for a foreign company may qualify as Russian-source income. Special rules also apply to remote employment and online services. Consider the contract, place of work, nature of the income and payer's status.

If you regularly provide services independently, check whether you need to register as a business. For a registered individual entrepreneur, or sole trader (IP), the applicable tax regime may differ from personal income tax.

Transferring your own money does not create new income

Not every credit to a bank account constitutes taxable income. Transferring previously accumulated funds between your own accounts does not in itself create new income. However, keep documents establishing the origin of the funds and check whether the original income was declared where required.

Personal income tax rates and filing deadlines

Since 2025, a progressive scale has applied to a resident's main tax base, including ordinary salary income.

Portion of the annual tax baseRate
Up to RUB 2.4 million13%
Over RUB 2.4 million up to RUB 5 million15%
Over RUB 5 million up to RUB 20 million18%
Over RUB 20 million up to RUB 50 million20%
Over RUB 50 million22%

The higher rate applies only to the corresponding portion of the tax base. Other rates and rules apply to particular types of income. For example, if the main tax base is RUB 3 million and there are no deductions, tax under this scale amounts to RUB 402,000.

Where income must be declared personally, the general deadline for filing a 3-NDFL tax return for 2026 income is 30 April 2027, and the payment deadline is 15 July 2027. Foreign-currency income is converted using the official Bank of Russia exchange rate on the date the income is actually received, in accordance with the Tax Code.

Do you need to notify the tax authority about overseas accounts?

As a general rule, a currency resident must notify the Federal Tax Service (FNS) of opening or closing an account, or changing its details, at a foreign bank or other financial market institution within one month of the relevant event. Exceptions and separate rules upon return apply to individuals who spend more than 183 days abroad during a calendar year.

If you opened an account before obtaining your Russian VNZh, assess separately the obligations arising when you acquire currency resident status. Tax residence alone does not create an obligation to notify the authorities of an account under currency control legislation.

Reporting movements of money and financial assets

For individual currency residents, the general deadline for submitting an annual report on overseas accounts is 1 June of the following year. A separate deadline applies when an account is closed. Accounts used by sole traders for business purposes are subject to separate quarterly reporting, generally within 30 days after the end of the quarter.

Exceptions may apply to private individuals. For example, a report on an account held in a Eurasian Economic Union (EAEU) country or a country participating in automatic financial information exchange may not be required if the statutory threshold is met: amounts credited and debited do not exceed RUB 600,000 annually, or the closing balance does not exceed that amount if there were no credits during the year. This exception does not automatically remove the obligation to notify the tax authority of opening the account.

The list of countries participating in automatic exchange changes. Separate rules apply to foreign electronic wallets. Check the legality of the specific currency transactions as well as reporting obligations.

What if you have already paid tax abroad?

Paying tax in another country does not automatically exempt you from Russian personal income tax. Whether a foreign tax credit is available depends on the applicable international treaty and Article 232 of the Russian Tax Code. Certain provisions of some treaties have been suspended, so check the particular country, type of income and relevant period.

Keep documents establishing your income and the foreign tax paid. Where necessary, prepare the confirmations and translations prescribed by law. If a credit is available, its amount is determined under the applicable rules.

What to prepare for tax and immigration purposes

Keep contracts, bank statements, payment confirmations and evidence of foreign taxes paid. Maintain a record of incoming payments showing the date, currency, amount and legal basis. Separate personal transfers from business revenue.

A bank may request evidence of the source of funds even where the transfer itself is not taxable. Separately check the income documentation requirements for temporary and permanent residence: a tax return does not automatically establish compliance with every immigration criterion. Key takeaway

If you live in Russia and receive money from abroad, first determine your tax and currency residence, then classify your income and assess your tax return and overseas account reporting obligations.
