DOING BUSINESS IN RUSSIA

Setting up a business in Russia

We help foreign entrepreneurs and companies choose the right business structure, register as a sole trader or establish a limited liability company, and begin operating legally in Russia.

Sole trader or limited liability company: which is right for you?

The choice depends on your business activities, immigration status, ownership structure, taxes, financial risks and growth plans.

Sole trader (individual entrepreneur, IP)

Suitable for independent business activity and many types of small business.

  • No separate legal entity is required.
  • Foreign nationals must have the right to reside temporarily or permanently in Russia.
  • As a general rule, a sole trader is personally liable for business obligations, except in respect of assets protected from enforcement by law.
Learn more about sole trader registration for foreign nationals →

Limited liability company (LLC; Russian OOO)

Suitable for establishing a separate company, working with partners and growing a business.

  • One or more members, including foreign persons.
  • As a general rule, a foreign founder does not need a temporary residence permit (RVP) or permanent residence permit (VNZh) to establish an LLC.
  • The company is liable for its obligations with its own assets. Statutory exceptions to members' limited liability apply.

STRATEGY FIRST, IMPLEMENTATION SECOND

There is more to starting a business than registration

Before registration, we determine how your business will operate: who will own and manage it, how payments will be arranged, and what taxes and authorisations must be considered.

The right to own a company, work for it and live in Russia are separate legal matters. Foreign owners or directors should align these with their immigration strategy in advance.

If you already operate a business outside Russia, we can assess options for entering the Russian market, including establishing a subsidiary or accrediting a branch or representative office.

Special considerations for foreign founders

When establishing a business with foreign participation, it is important to check founders' documents and authority, restrictions applicable to particular persons and activities, special payment rules and requirements for the company's director.

Where a foreign organisation is a founder, we consider the requirements for proving its legal status, preparing documents and translating them into Russian.

We examine these matters before filing registration documents so the chosen structure fits your actual plans.

Frequently asked questions

Can a foreign national register as a sole trader in Russia?

Yes, if they have the right to reside temporarily or permanently in Russia (generally an RVP or VNZh) and meet the other registration requirements. An ordinary visa or migration card alone is not enough.

Can a foreign national establish an LLC without a residence permit?

As a general rule, a foreign individual or legal entity may establish an LLC. A temporary or permanent residence permit is not a general registration requirement for foreign founders. Restrictions applicable to the particular participant and business activity must still be checked.

Do I need a Russian business partner?

Generally, no. An LLC may be established by a single foreign member, provided no special restrictions apply to that member or the proposed business activity.

Can a foreign founder also be the director?

Yes, if the applicable requirements for appointment, immigration status and the right to work are met. These matters must be assessed separately from company registration.

Does setting up a business entitle me to a residence permit?

Establishing a company or registering as a sole trader does not in itself confer immigration status. We assess independent routes to residence, including investment-based routes where applicable.

Can you help after registration?

Yes. We can assist with opening a bank account, contracts, corporate matters, working with your accountant and ongoing legal support.
